Every preflop decision in the session was scored against Jeff Hwang’s Big Play Strategy framework: each starting hand is valued on pair equity, nut-flush/suit equity and straight-wrap connectedness, less dangler and dead-card penalties, then sorted into five playability tiers. Your action is compared with what the framework prescribes for that effective seat and that facing-action.

Errors are weighted by cost. Playing a hand the framework folds scores 0; folding too tight or limping a hand that should raise scores 0.5, because those are the cheaper mistakes. The strict match figure above is the unweighted exact-match rate.

The read: hand selection is sound — you fold 98.7% of trash and play 93.3% of premiums, which is what holds the score up. Hand actions are the leak: too passive with the top of your range, too loose from the small blind. Tightening the SB to roughly your cutoff opening tier and simply raising every KK+ would put this near 95%.

Seats are graded by effective position — players left to act behind you — not the raw label, since these tables ran 2 to 7 handed and a 4-handed “UTG” is really a cutoff. The small blind is the weak seat.

Error Profile

Where the points go

The most expensive leak is playing hands the framework folds — and half of it sits in one seat. Eight of the sixteen loose plays are the small blind, five of them continuing against a raise, out of position, with speculative hands like 7d 3d 7s 5s and 4s 6s 8c 7h. Those need position or a pair to be worth a flop.

The more interesting leak is the opposite one. VPIP 20.8% against PFR 7.5% is a 13-point gap: you fold and limp genuinely strong hands. Four separate KKxx hands were folded or limped, plus two QQxx — and Ac Ad 7c Ks was limped on the button. Of 39 premium hands dealt, 9 were not raised.

The Tiers

How hands are graded

  • T1Premium. Raise and 3-bet from any seat. AAxx with a suit or connector, KKxx double-suited, high double-suited rundowns.
  • T2Strong. Open from any seat, continue against a raise. Big pairs with support, mid rundowns double-suited.
  • T3Speculative. Needs late position, a multiway pot, or a big-blind price to be worth playing.
  • T4Marginal. Button steal or a free big-blind flop only.
  • T5Trash. Fold. Danglers, non-nut suits, disconnected low cards, dead trips.

Method & Caveats

How to read this page

Calibration

Hwang’s book gives categories and principles, not a hand-by-hand chart. The tier thresholds and positional opening ranges here are one calibration of his framework. The relative rankings are faithful to him; the exact cutoff between, say, a tier-3 and a tier-4 hand is a judgment call. Treat the error patterns as the signal and the percentage as an index, not a solver verdict.

Scope

Preflop only — hand selection and the chosen action. Raise sizing, postflop play and opponent tendencies are not scored. 346 of 355 hands produced a preflop decision; the other 9 folded through without action on Hero. Position, action and facing-raise detection were audited against the raw histories.

Hands are valued as pair equity + suit equity + connectedness − penalties. Pair equity scales steeply (AA = 10 down to 22 = 0.5). Suit equity rewards the nut suit (2.5) far above a low suit (1.0), with a bonus for double-suited and a discount for a redundant third card of a suit. Connectedness sums every two-card straight combination, weighted up for nut-end broadway cards and down for bottom-end traps, with the ace counted both high and low for wheel value. Danglers cost 1.5; trips cost 3.0 for the dead card.
